A sewer line repair replaces or restores the part of the pipe that is cracked, offset, collapsed or full of roots. Depending on the damage, that may be a short dig, a full replacement, or a trenchless liner pulled through the existing pipe.

When a sewer problem cannot wait

A partly collapsed line may still drain slowly for days, then close suddenly. Sewage in the basement, a sinkhole or soft spot over the line in the yard, and a persistent sewer smell outdoors are all reasons for a same-day call.

While you wait, stop laundry and showers, since every gallon has to go somewhere. If the backup has already reached the floor, wear gloves and boots, ventilate the room and avoid contact with the water. Bacteria in sewage are the real hazard, and cleaning belongs to people with proper equipment.

Dig and replace compared with trenchless lining

The right fix depends on the damage, the yard and the pipe material.

Open-trench repair or replacement

  • Best when a section has collapsed or lost its slope
  • Replaces the pipe with new PVC
  • Needs excavation and yard restoration

Trenchless lining

  • A resin liner cures inside the existing pipe
  • Less digging, and the landscape stays largely in place
  • Needs a pipe that is still structurally continuous and passable
