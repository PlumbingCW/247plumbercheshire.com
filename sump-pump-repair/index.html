A sump pump sits in a pit in the lowest part of the basement. When water fills the pit, a float switch turns the pump on and pushes water out through a discharge pipe, away from the foundation. Sump pump repair fixes the float, the motor, the check valve and the discharge line.

Pump not working in a storm: what to do

Check the simple things first. Confirm the pump is plugged in and the outlet works, which may be a GFCI that tripped. Lift the float to see whether the pump starts. Look for debris jamming the float, and check that the discharge pipe is not frozen or kinked.

If water is rising and the pump will not run, bail or wet-vacuum what you can, and call. Do not touch electrical equipment while standing in water. Turn off the breaker for the basement if the water is near outlets, and only if you can reach the panel dry.

Why pumps fail

Most failures are mechanical and predictable.

The float is stuck

The switch rides against the pit wall or a pipe and never rises.

The motor burned out

Pumps that run dry or cycle constantly wear out. Many last around a decade.

The check valve failed

Water falls back into the pit after each cycle, so the pump cycles often.

The discharge is blocked

A frozen or clogged line forces water back.

Power outage

A pump without a backup stops when the power does.

Why Cheshire basements need working pumps

Cheshire sits in New Haven County with rivers and brooks that rise after heavy rain, and low-lying areas near water get wet basements first. The town runs on a mix of public water, private wells and septic, and where homes sit on a high water table the sump is the main defense. We do not state local groundwater depth, because it varies by lot.

If your sump pump runs often even in dry weather, that points to a high water table or a leaking pipe. A pump that never runs may mean the pit is not collecting water, and a test with a bucket of water shows whether it starts. Test it every season.

Primary pump and battery backup

A backup protects you when the main pump fails or the power goes out.

Primary pump

  • Runs on house power
  • Handles routine water
  • Fails if the power is out

Battery or water-powered backup

  • Runs when the primary fails or power is lost
  • Smaller capacity than the main pump
  • Needs periodic battery checks

Code and permits for sump pump work

Connecticut plumbing work falls under the 2022 State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Where a pump discharges is regulated, so a new discharge line is worth checking with the town first. Permit questions go to the Cheshire Building Department at 203-271-6640, since the Building Official issues plumbing permits in town.

Discharge, drainage and nearby fixes

Water should discharge well away from the foundation. A discharge that ends near the house recirculates water into the same wall, and a discharge into a septic area can overload the system. If the basement is wet even with a working pump, look at drainage and basement waterproofing for a longer-term fix. A pump line that has frozen and split is covered under burst pipe repair.
