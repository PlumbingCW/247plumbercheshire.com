Commercial Plumber in Cheshire's North End, CT

The North End is Cheshire's industrial and commercial area along the northern end of town. This page is for businesses and building managers there. It is not about a residential neighborhood.

Cheshire's plan describes the North End Industrial Corridor as the industrial district along the northern end of town and says it acts in many ways as the economic powerhouse of the town. It is also a focus for economic development and for new multi-family housing, which the plan expects along Route 10 between Realty Drive and the Southington town line.

That makes this a commercial page, not a neighborhood page. It is for owners, managers and tenants of shops, plants, offices and apartment buildings in the North End. Homeowners should start with the main Cheshire page.

Drains, grease and floor drains in a commercial building

A commercial drain problem often starts with what goes down the line. Kitchens send grease. Shops send oils and grit. Plants send process water and debris.

The town's Water Pollution Control Division runs a Fats, Oils and Grease management program, so a food business should ask the division what applies before changing a kitchen drain. A clogged line can be cleared by drain cleaning or hydro jetting, and a line that keeps clogging deserves a camera look through a sewer inspection.

A floor drain that backs up in a shop or a warehouse is a drainage problem, not just a nuisance. Stop adding water and keep people out of the wet area. Then call and describe where the water is and what is in it.

Apartment buildings and shared lines

Multi-family buildings add shared stacks and shared shutoffs. A leak in one unit can affect the unit below, and the shutoff you need may be in a basement room that tenants cannot reach. Managers should label the main and the unit shutoffs and tell tenants where they are. When a hidden leak is suspected, leak detection looks for it without opening every wall.

New construction, connections and what the town decides

The plan says water and sewer availability can limit development in the North End, so confirm what is available before you plan a new connection. The town has a page on sewer connections for new construction, and Town Hall at 203-271-6600 can point you to the right office.

Permits for plumbing work come from the Cheshire Building Department at 203-271-6640, under the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code and the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) it adopts, and the Building Official decides what applies. We do not make rules for the town, and we will not guess at them.

Before you call about a business plumbing problem

These steps help in almost any commercial building.

Find the shutoff

Locate the main water shutoff for the building and for your unit. Close it if water is running freely.

Protect people

Keep staff and customers away from wet floors and from any area where drains have backed up.

Protect stock and equipment

Move what you can out of the wet area. Do not touch wet electrical equipment.

Call

Call (203) 720-6269 and describe the building and the problem. Ask about timing and what the visit will include.

Neighboring areas

The northern edge of Cheshire touches Southington, which has its own water and sewer departments. If your building is on the Southington side of the line, that town's offices apply, and our Southington page lists them.
