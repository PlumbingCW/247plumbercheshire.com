Plumbing installation in Cheshire, CT covers adding or replacing fixtures and the lines that serve them. Our line is open 24/7, though most installs are scheduled visits. Call any time to describe the project, and we will set a walk-through.

Installation work includes sinks, tubs, showers, toilets, laundry hookups, outdoor spigots, water filtration connections and rough plumbing for a remodel or addition. Good installs are accessible, protected against freezing and ready for inspection.

What goes into a clean install

The visible part is the fixture. The work that matters is behind it: correctly sized supply lines, proper venting for drains, slope on the waste pipe, shutoff valves that work and fittings that are accessible. A cheap shortcut here shows up later as slow drains, noise or leaks.

We confirm what the project needs before ordering parts, including fixture specifications, rough-in dimensions and whether the existing water and drain pipes can support the new load. If they cannot, we say so and give options.

Permits and inspections in Cheshire

Many installations need a permit. The Cheshire Building Department at 84 South Main Street issues building and plumbing permits through its Building Official, and projects often involve more than one kind of permit. Permit rules vary, so call 203-271-6640 or ask us what we expect applies to your project.

If your property is on septic rather than the sewer, adding a bathroom or fixture can affect the septic system. The Chesprocott Health District, serving Cheshire, Prospect and Wolcott, handles those questions, including additions. Check with them before adding fixtures to a septic home.

Planning a remodel: bring the plumber in early

The best time to involve a plumber is before walls close. Moving a toilet or a tub drain affects the slope of waste pipes and the vent, and a few inches of framing can decide whether a layout works. A short walk-through before the design is final saves expensive changes later.

Order fixtures before rough-in. Valves, drains and supply lines sit at set heights that depend on the fixture, and a changed faucet can mean opening a finished wall. Keep the specification sheets, because the plumber needs the rough-in dimensions. After the rough-in, pressure test the lines and have the inspection done before the drywall goes up.

Basement bathrooms and ejector pumps

A bathroom below the level of the main drain needs a way to lift waste up to the sewer line, and that is an ejector pump in a sealed pit. It is a standard solution, and it has to be vented and fitted with a check valve. It also needs an alarm, because a failed pump in a basement bathroom is a messy day.

If you are on septic, adding a basement bathroom adds load. Ask the Chesprocott Health District before you design it.
