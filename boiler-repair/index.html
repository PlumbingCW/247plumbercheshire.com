Emergency boiler repair in Cheshire, CT is the call to make when the house goes cold and the radiators or baseboards stay lukewarm. Our 24/7 line is open all winter. Check that the thermostat is calling for heat, the boiler switch is on and the fuel supply is available, then call.

A boiler heats water and circulates it through baseboards, radiators or radiant loops. Repair covers the burner, the controls, the circulator pumps, valves, the expansion tank and leaks in the boiler or piping.

No heat in cold weather: what to do first

Check the thermostat settings and batteries, the switch on or near the boiler, and the oil level or gas supply. Look at the pressure gauge. Most hydronic systems sit within a modest pressure range, and a gauge near zero means water is missing.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or Yankee Gas at 877-944-5325. If you smell oil fumes or see soot, call us and leave the boiler off. A boiler leaking water should be shut off and the water supply to it closed. Keep the house warm in the meantime, and protect pipes in exterior walls from freezing.

What the symptoms mean

Boilers tell you what is wrong in familiar ways.

No heat, boiler silent

A tripped switch, a lockout, a failed control or no fuel.

Banging or kettling noises

Scale or air in the system, or a failing pump.

Radiators cold on upper floors

Trapped air. Bleeding the radiators or the system may help.

Water around the boiler

A leaking valve, a failed relief valve or a cracked heat exchanger.

Pressure keeps dropping

A leak somewhere in the loop, or a failed expansion tank.

Older heating systems in Cheshire homes

Many Cheshire homes heat with a hydronic boiler fed by natural gas or oil. A boiler can last a long time with annual service, and older units are common in houses built decades ago. Annual service, including burner cleaning and controls, extends life and catches problems before January.

We do not guess the age of your boiler from the house. Look at the data plate for the make and model. If a boiler is leaking from the heat exchanger or has had repeated repairs, replacement is a conversation for after the emergency is handled. In the meantime, keep the heat on in the coldest rooms and open cabinet doors on exterior walls.

Keeping a hydronic system healthy

Bleed air from radiators at the start of the heating season, and check that furniture and curtains do not block baseboard fins. Look at the pressure gauge once a month. A reading that creeps up or down from its normal range is an early warning that something needs attention.

Keep the area around the boiler clear and dry, and never store paint, solvents or cleaning products next to it. Annual service by a qualified technician cleans the burner, checks the controls and tests safety devices. It costs far less than a no-heat call on the coldest night of the year.
