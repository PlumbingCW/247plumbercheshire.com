Emergency burst pipe repair in Cheshire, CT starts before we arrive. Turn off the main water shutoff, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines, and call our 24/7 line. A burst pipe can release a large volume of water in an hour, so shutting the main is the single most useful thing you can do.

Burst pipe repair means stopping the water, finding the split or failed fitting, cutting out the damaged section and replacing it with sound pipe. Frozen pipes that have not yet split are a related emergency, and thawing them correctly can prevent the burst.

The first ten minutes

Do these in order. They limit damage before a plumber is on site.

  • 1

    Shut off the main. It is usually where the line enters the basement. Turn it fully clockwise.

  • 2

    Drain the lines. Open the lowest cold tap and a hot tap to relieve pressure.

  • 3

    Kill power in wet areas. Only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Otherwise wait.

  • 4

    Move belongings and photograph. Document damage for insurance, then call (203) 720-6269.

Frozen pipes in Connecticut winters

Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages, crawlspaces and attics are the usual victims during a hard cold snap. A frozen pipe often announces itself with a trickle or no water at all from one tap. Do not use an open flame to thaw it, since heat applied too fast or too close can start a fire or split the pipe.

Open the faucet so melting ice can escape, warm the area gently with a hair dryer or by raising the room temperature, and call a plumber if you cannot find the frozen section. If you see a split or a bulge, leave it and shut off the main, because the water will pour out as soon as the ice melts.

What a burst looks like

The first signs are often small.

A sudden loss of water pressure

A split pipe loses pressure fast. Check taps on every floor.

Water stains spreading on a ceiling

Water moves along framing and shows where it finds a low spot.

The sound of running water

A pressurized leak can be heard through a wall.

A bulge or frost on a pipe

The ice inside is about to split the pipe or already has.

Repair options by pipe type

The material decides how the fix is made.

PipeTypical repair
CopperCut out the split, solder new copper and fittings, or use press fittings.
PEXCut the damaged length and join with crimp or expansion fittings.
Galvanized steelOften replaced in sections, and old galvanized may be worth a full repiping plan.
CPVCReplace the cracked section. Old CPVC can be brittle.

Cheshire homes and cold-weather risks

Cheshire winters are cold enough that an unheated garage or an addition over a crawlspace is a real risk. Homes near open fields along Route 10 and Route 68 can face stronger wind on exposed walls. Insulate exposed pipes, keep a trickle running on a pipe you worry about in a deep freeze, and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.
