Tankless water heater service in Cheshire, CT covers three jobs: installing a new unit, flushing mineral scale from a running one, and repairing faults. Our line is answered 24/7, so call any time a tankless heater stops delivering hot water. Write down the error code on the display first, since it often points straight at the problem.

A tankless heater warms water only when a tap runs, using a gas burner or electric elements and a heat exchanger. That saves floor space and avoids standby loss, and it makes the heat exchanger the part that needs care.

Why tankless units need a yearly flush

Water carries dissolved minerals, and heating it leaves scale on the inside of the heat exchanger. Scale narrows the water path, so the unit works harder, throws error codes, or cuts out. A flush circulates a descaling solution, such as vinegar or a manufacturer-approved cleaner, through the heat exchanger and then rinses it out.

We do not quote a local hardness number, because the Regional Water Authority's report does not print one for Cheshire. Test your tap, or ask the utility for the annual water quality report. In harder water the flush interval is shorter, and many manufacturers recommend a yearly flush as a baseline.

Signs a tankless heater needs attention

Most faults show up in the same few ways.

Water runs cold or flips between hot and cold

The flow may be below the minimum the unit needs, or scale may be restricting the heat exchanger.

An error code appears

Codes point to ignition, venting, sensor or flow issues. Photograph it before you call.

Fan noise or a clicking ignition that fails

A venting or combustion airflow problem, or a dirty burner.

Reduced flow at taps

A clogged inlet filter or scale in the heat exchanger.

Tankless or tank for a Cheshire home

Neither is right for everyone. These are the real trade-offs we discuss.

Tank water heater

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Works with the existing gas line and vent
  • Hot water in reserve during a power outage on gas models

Tankless water heater

  • Smaller footprint and no standby tank loss
  • Endless hot water at the unit's flow limit
  • Often needs a larger gas line, special venting and a condensate drain

Installing a tankless unit in an older Cheshire house

Gas capacity is the first check. A tankless unit draws much more gas at once than a tank heater, and a house that has run on a small line may need an upgrade. The venting is the second check, since many units use plastic vent pipe that has to run through a wall or the roof.

Permit rules vary. The Cheshire Building Department at 84 South Main Street issues plumbing permits, and a licensed plumber can tell you what your project needs.
