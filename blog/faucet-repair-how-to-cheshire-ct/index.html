How to Repair a Faucet in Cheshire, CT

Most dripping faucets need one inexpensive part. Here is how to identify the type, swap the part and decide when to call.

24/7 emergency line
(203) 720-6269
Call now

By the Cheshire 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

A dripping faucet is almost always a worn part, not a failed faucet. The part is usually a cartridge, a washer, a seat or an O-ring, and replacing it takes under an hour on most models. Start by shutting off the water at the valves under the sink.

The steps depend on your faucet type. Identify the type first, then follow the matching repair. If you hit a seized valve or a corroded fitting, stop and call a plumber.

Before you start

Prepare in this order.

  • 1

    Shut off the water. Close the stop valves under the sink. If they will not turn, shut the main.

  • 2

    Plug the drain. A small part dropped down the drain is a pain. Use a rag or plug.

  • 3

    Open the faucet. Drain the remaining water and relieve pressure.

  • 4

    Take a photo. Photograph the faucet before disassembly so you can reassemble it correctly.

Four common faucet types

The handle style hints at the internal parts.

Compression faucet

Two handles that tighten down. Worn rubber washers and seats are the usual culprit.

Cartridge faucet

One or two handles that move smoothly. A worn cartridge or O-rings causes drips.

Ball faucet

A single handle on a ball. Worn seats, springs and the ball itself leak.

Ceramic disc faucet

A single lever with ceramic discs. Replace the disc cartridge when it leaks.

The basic repair for a cartridge faucet

Pry off the handle cap, remove the screw and lift off the handle. Unscrew the retaining nut or clip, and pull the cartridge straight out. Compare it to a new one and match the size and stem. Push the new cartridge in, line up the notch or tab and reassemble.

Turn the water on slowly and check for leaks at the base and the spout. If water sprays, shut it off and recheck the alignment. If the cartridge is stuck, a puller tool helps, and forcing it with pliers can crack the body.

Low flow and mineral buildup

A faucet with a weak stream may only need a clean aerator. Unscrew it from the spout, soak it in vinegar for an hour and rinse. If the stream is still weak, the cartridge may be scaled and need replacing. Scale builds wherever heated water sits, and it is the usual cause of stiff handles too.

A drip at the spout that stops when you tighten the handle is a washer or seat. Seats can be resurfaced or replaced, and a worn seat will eat a new washer within weeks.

Cheshire note: water and what to ask

Cheshire's water comes from the Regional Water Authority. Its annual report does not state a hardness figure for the town, so we do not guess one. If aerators clog often at your house, test your tap with a hardness kit and ask the utility for its water quality report.

Plumbing permits are not usually needed for simple repairs, though rules vary, and the Cheshire Building Department at 203-271-6640 can confirm for larger work.

A quick decision guide.

SituationBest choice

Stop valves will not close or leakCall a plumber, since a broken valve floods the cabinet
Water under the sinkCheck supply lines. Call if you cannot find it
Corroded fittings or a wall-mounted faucetPlumber

Tools and parts for a first repair

A short toolbox handles most faucet repairs: an adjustable wrench, a set of screwdrivers, a hex key set, pliers, a flashlight, plumber's grease and a small bucket. Add penetrating oil for stubborn nuts and a cartridge puller if your faucet uses one.

Buy the replacement part after you pull the old one, and take it to the store to match. Brand names are stamped on the body or under the handle. Keep every small part in a cup so nothing rolls down the drain, and wrap the old parts in a bag so you can compare them with the new ones.

Kitchen faucets, bath faucets and pull-down sprayers

Kitchen faucets with a pull-down spray head add a hose and a weight on the supply line. If the spray head drips or the hose does not retract, the problem may be the hose, the weight or the quick connect, not the cartridge. Check those before pulling the cartridge.

Bathroom faucets with a pop-up drain have a lift rod and a pivot that can leak under the sink. A drip from the cabinet may come from the pivot nut, not the faucet. Following the water with a dry paper towel finds the real source. If you cannot trace it, call a plumber rather than replacing parts at random.

Replacing the supply lines at the same time

If the faucet is old, the supply lines under it are old too. Braided stainless lines age, and a failure floods a cabinet quickly. Replacing them during a faucet repair costs little and removes a risk.

Use lines with the correct length and connection size for your valves and faucet. Hand tighten, then add a quarter turn with a wrench. Do not overtighten, since the washers seal on their own. Turn the water on slowly and check each joint with a dry tissue.

Permits and code for faucet repair

The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code is built on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). A faucet swap is routine work, while new supply lines can fall under permit rules. If your job might need a permit, call the Cheshire Building Department at 203-271-6640 before work starts.

When to call a plumber

If a valve is seized, a handle is stuck or the faucet body is corroded, stop. A plumber can replace the shutoff valves and the faucet, and our faucet repair page lists what we handle. If water is spreading under the sink, close the main and call right away at (203) 720-6269.

If you see water coming from a ceiling below, a hidden leak may be involved, and leak detection can trace it. We serve Southington and Wallingford too.
