Leaking Toilet Repair in Cheshire, CT

A toilet can leak in four places, and each has a different fix. Here is how to find yours before it damages the floor.

24/7 emergency line
(203) 720-6269

By the Cheshire 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

If your toilet is leaking, the first move is to close the small valve on the wall behind it. That stops the supply, and it gives you time to work out where the water comes from. Most toilet leaks fall into four groups: the tank, the supply line, the base and the inside of the bowl where water slips past a worn flapper.

The right repair depends on which one you have, and a quick dye test and a dry paper towel can tell you in ten minutes. This guide walks through the checks in the order a plumber would use them.

Four checks that find the leak

Do them in this order, drying the surfaces between checks.

  • 1

    Check the supply line and valve. Wipe the connection at the wall and at the tank. Press a dry tissue to each joint. A damp tissue means a loose nut or worn washer.

  • 2

    Check the tank bolts and the tank edge. Water drips from the bolts that hold the tank to the bowl, or from a hairline crack. Tighten bolts gently, since porcelain cracks.

  • 3

    Check the base. Water around the base after a flush points to a failed wax ring or a loose bolt. Rocking the toilet is the tell.

  • 4

    Run the dye test. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows in the bowl after 15 minutes without flushing, the flapper leaks.

Leaks inside the bowl: the quiet water waster

A flapper that no longer seals lets tank water trickle into the bowl, and the fill valve then refills the tank over and over. You may hear the toilet hiss or run for a few seconds every few minutes. It leaves no puddle, so it is easy to ignore, and it can add real water to the monthly bill.

Replacing a flapper takes a few minutes and costs little. If the chain is too short or too long, it can hold the flapper open, so adjust that first. If the fill valve keeps running after the tank is full, replace the valve as well.

Leaks at the base: why they cause damage

A wax ring seals the toilet to the drain flange. When it fails, water seeps out at the floor on every flush. The leak is often small, so it goes unnoticed until the floor softens or a stain shows on the ceiling below.

Do not just caulk around the base. The fix is to lift the toilet, replace the wax ring, check the flange for cracks and set the toilet back on new bolts. If the flange is broken or sits below the floor, it needs repair. We cover that detail in our guide to toilet repair on the service page.

When to call a plumber

Call if you see a crack in the porcelain, if the toilet rocks and the floor feels soft, or if the base keeps leaking after a new wax ring. Also call if a leak recurs, since the flange or the drain may be at fault. A toilet that backs up while leaking points past the fixture, and our drain cleaning team can check the line.

Parts worth having on hand

A basic repair kit covers most toilet leaks: a universal flapper, a fill valve, a set of tank bolts with rubber washers, a braided supply line and a wax ring with new closet bolts. None is costly, and keeping them in a basement cabinet turns a weekend emergency into a short job.

Match the parts to your toilet. Fill valves and flappers come in several sizes, and the wrong flapper will not seal. Take a photo of the tank interior and the brand name stamped inside the tank when you shop. If the toilet is old and parts are scarce, that is a sign the toilet may be due for replacement.

Mistakes that make a leak worse

Overtightening is the classic one. Tank bolts and closet bolts sit on porcelain, and a quarter turn too far can crack the tank or the base, which turns a ten dollar repair into a new toilet. Tighten gently, alternate sides and stop when the part is snug.

Another mistake is sealing the base with caulk and calling it fixed. Caulk can hide a leak that keeps feeding the subfloor. Leave a gap at the back if you caulk at all, so any future leak shows. And do not keep flushing a toilet that is leaking at the base, since each flush pushes more water onto the floor.

Code and permits for toilet repairs

Connecticut plumbing work falls under the 2022 State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). A wax ring or flange fix is routine, and moving a toilet is a bigger job. Permit questions go to the Cheshire Building Department at 203-271-6640, since the Building Official issues plumbing permits in town.

A note on water bills

A silent leak can add meaningful water over a month. Check your meter with all fixtures off. If the meter moves, look at toilets first, then outdoor spigots and irrigation. The dye test takes minutes and often explains the whole problem.

The repair itself is among the least costly in plumbing, so there is little reason to wait. A leaking toilet gets worse, and the damage is in the floor and subfloor, not the fixture.
