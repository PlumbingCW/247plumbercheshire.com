Burst Pipe Repair for Cheshire, CT Homes

When a pipe bursts, the first ten minutes decide how much damage you get. Here is what to do and what the repair involves.

24/7 emergency line
(203) 720-6269
Call now

By the Cheshire 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

When a pipe bursts, the first step is to shut off the main water valve. The second is to open the lowest faucet to drain the lines. The third is to call a plumber. Those three moves limit damage before anyone arrives.

This guide covers the first steps, the usual causes and what a repair looks like. It also covers what to document for your insurer, and how to prepare for the next cold snap.

The first ten minutes

Do these in order.

  • 1

    Shut off the main. Turn the valve fully closed. Know where it is before an emergency.

  • 2

    Drain the lines. Open the lowest cold tap and a hot tap.

  • 3

    Cut power where water pools. Only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

  • 4

    Document. Photograph the damage and the pipe before cleanup.

  • 5

    Call. Dial (203) 720-6269 and describe the location and extent.

Why pipes burst

Freezing is the best-known cause. Water expands as it freezes, and the pressure splits the pipe, usually at the point where the ice blocks the line, not where it froze. Corrosion and age are the other major causes, especially in old galvanized and thin copper pipe. High water pressure and a failed pressure regulator can stress a pipe until it splits.

A burst from freezing often shows up after the thaw, when ice melts and water flows through the split. That is why a pipe that froze overnight may only reveal itself in the afternoon.

Where bursts usually happen

Know the weak spots in your house.

Exterior walls

Pipes behind a cold wall with little insulation.

Unheated garages and crawlspaces

Lines that run through spaces without heat.

Attic runs

Supply lines under cold roof decking.

Outdoor spigots

A hose left attached can freeze back into the wall.

Connecticut winters bring repeated freeze and thaw, and exposed walls on open lots feel it most. If your shutoff valve will not close, call the Regional Water Authority about the curb stop, and call us for the plumbing. The utility's annual report notes that lead in drinking water mostly comes from service lines and home plumbing, a reminder to ask what your pipes are made of before you repair them.

Preventing the next one

Insulate pipes in unheated spaces with foam sleeves. Seal gaps where cold air enters near pipes. Keep cabinet doors open under sinks on exterior walls during a deep freeze, and let a faucet trickle if you worry about a line. Disconnect hoses in the fall and shut off the interior valve to outdoor spigots.

If a pipe has burst twice, the pipe is the problem. Ask about replacing that run, and consider repiping if the system is old. A frozen pipe that has not split can be thawed gently with a hair dryer.

Water damage: the first 48 hours

After the water is off and the pipe is repaired, drying becomes the priority. Remove standing water, pull wet rugs and furniture, run fans and dehumidifiers and open windows when the weather allows. Wet drywall and insulation hold moisture, and mold can start growing within a couple of days.

For a small leak you can handle drying yourself. For a large one, a water damage specialist has equipment to dry walls and floors fast. Keep photographs and a written list of what was damaged, and keep damaged items until your insurer says you may dispose of them.

Choosing a repair that lasts

A burst caused by a single freeze may need only a short section replaced. A burst caused by age or corrosion often means more weak pipe nearby. A plumber can look along the run and point out the next likely failure, which is helpful when the walls are already open.

If you are going to open a wall anyway, it is the right time to improve things: insulate the pipe, move it away from the cold exterior wall if the framing allows, or add a shutoff valve for that branch. Small upgrades made during a repair are inexpensive compared with a second burst.

Pipes in unheated spaces: a quick audit

Walk the house this fall and list every pipe that runs through a cold space: garage walls, crawlspaces, attic runs, exterior walls and outdoor spigots. Mark them on a sketch. That list tells you where to put insulation, where to open cabinet doors and where to check first after a cold night.

Heat tape is an option for specific vulnerable runs, and it needs to be installed and used according to the maker's directions. A plumber can reroute or insulate a worst-case line before winter, which is far cheaper than a burst.

When to call right away

Call at once if water is spreading, if the shutoff will not close, if the pipe is in a ceiling or if power outlets are wet. Call also if the burst is in an exterior wall in freezing weather, since the repair has to be done before the house cools.

If you are not sure whether a stain is a burst or an old leak, call anyway. A short phone conversation tells us whether it can wait for a regular visit.

Insurance, drying and who does what

Many policies cover sudden bursts and the damage they cause. Photograph the area, keep receipts and keep any damaged material until the adjuster looks. Water damage specialists handle drying and mold prevention, and we handle the pipe.
