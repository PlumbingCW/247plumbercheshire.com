Gas Water Heater Venting in Cheshire, CT

Venting carries combustion gases outdoors. A bad vent is a safety problem, which is why this is not a corner to cut.

24/7 emergency line
(203) 720-6269
Call now

By the Cheshire 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

A gas water heater burns fuel and must send the exhaust outdoors. The vent does that job. Getting it right matters for safety, because combustion gases include carbon monoxide.

This guide covers the main vent types, what a plumber checks during an install and what homeowners should watch for. It does not replace the manufacturer's instructions or local code, which a licensed plumber follows.

The main vent types

Atmospheric vent water heaters use a draft hood and a metal vent pipe that rises to a chimney or a roof. Power vent heaters use a fan and plastic vent pipe that can exit through a side wall. Direct vent heaters use a sealed pipe pair that brings combustion air in and sends exhaust out. Condensing high-efficiency models use plastic vent pipe and need a condensate drain.

Each type has its own pipe material, length and slope rules. The manufacturer's installation manual gives the limits, and a plumber follows them.

Warning signs of a vent problem

Take any of these seriously.

Soot or scorch marks near the heater

Combustion gases are escaping into the room.

A pilot that keeps going out

Draft problems or a blocked vent can cause this.

Moisture or rust at the vent joints

Condensation in the flue indicates a venting issue.

A carbon monoxide alarm sounds

Leave the house and call 911. Do not reset and ignore it.

What a plumber checks during an install

We check the vent size and material against the manual, the slope and support of the vent pipe, the clearances from combustible material and the combustion air supply. On a replacement, we also look at the old vent for corrosion or blockage, since a vent that served the old heater may not suit the new one.

After the install we test for proper draft or fan operation, check for leaks at the joints and confirm the gas connection holds pressure. Our water heater installation team handles planned swaps, and water heater replacement covers emergencies.

Combustion air: the other half of the vent

A gas heater needs air to burn, as well as a path for exhaust. In a small closet or a tight basement room, the air supply can run short, and the flame draws exhaust back into the room. Louvered doors, vents to outside or sealed combustion models solve that.

A heater in a room with a clothes dryer, an exhaust fan or a fireplace can face competing airflow, which can pull exhaust backward. A plumber checks the room, watches the draft with the other appliances running and tells you if the setup needs changes.

Carbon monoxide detectors and annual checks

Install carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas and on every level, test them monthly and replace them as the maker advises. An alarm is a backup, and it does not substitute for a safe vent, though it can save lives when something fails.

Have the heater, the vent and the connections inspected as part of annual maintenance. Look at the vent joints for rust and the draft hood for soot. If you ever see scorch marks, rust streaks or a loose pipe, call a plumber. Do not wait for winter, when closed windows make problems more dangerous.

Replacing a vent when the heater changes

A new heater often needs a new vent, even when the old one looks fine. A modern high-efficiency heater may need plastic vent pipe, while an older atmospheric heater used metal. Swapping to a different model can change the vent diameter, length and routing.

Reusing an old vent that does not match is a common cause of poor draft. We check what the new heater requires, measure the run and replace what must be replaced. That approach avoids a repeat visit after the first cold night shows a problem.

Questions to ask before an install

Ask the installer which vent type the new heater uses and whether the existing vent can be reused. Ask how combustion air will be supplied, how the vent is supported and how it is tested after installation. Ask whether a permit is being pulled and who is responsible for it.

A clear answer to each question tells you the installer has thought about the job. Vague answers are a reason to keep looking. A heater is not an appliance to guess about, and a short list of questions protects your household.

Code and permits for water heater venting

Connecticut plumbing work falls under the 2022 State Building Code, which adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Venting is where the plumbing and fuel gas rules meet, which is why it is not a place for guesses. Permit questions go to the Cheshire Building Department at 203-271-6640, since the Building Official issues plumbing permits in town.

Why DIY venting is a poor idea

Vent mistakes are not visible until they hurt someone. A joint that is slightly loose, a slope that is wrong or an undersized vent can all push exhaust into the house. A carbon monoxide detector near sleeping areas is a necessary backup, and a plumber's test confirms the system works.
