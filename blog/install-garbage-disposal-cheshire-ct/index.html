Installing a Garbage Disposal in a Cheshire Home

Installing a disposal is a manageable job when the sink is set up for it. Here is what is involved, and what Cheshire homes should check first.

By the Cheshire 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

A garbage disposal installs under the kitchen sink, below the drain opening, and connects to the sink flange, the drain trap and often the dishwasher. If your sink already has a disposal, a replacement is mostly a swap. A new install adds an electrical connection and a switch.

Before you buy one, check whether a disposal makes sense for your home. In Cheshire, that starts with whether you are on public sewer or a septic system, since a disposal adds solids to whatever sits downstream.

Check septic and sewer first

The town Sewer Division reports serving about 5,000 properties, so many Cheshire homes are on the public sewer. Others use septic. A disposal sends more solids into a septic tank, and the tank fills faster. If you have septic, ask the Chesprocott Health District or your septic contractor whether a disposal fits your system, and expect to pump the tank more often.

If you are on sewer, a disposal is fine for small food scraps, but fats and grease still belong in the trash. Grease cools in the pipe and builds up over time.

What the installation involves

A plumber's sequence looks like this.

  • 1

    Prepare the sink flange. Remove the old strainer or flange. Set the new flange with plumber's putty and tighten the mounting ring.

  • 2

    Mount the disposal. Hang the unit on the mounting assembly and lock it in place.

  • 3

    Connect the drain. Attach the discharge elbow and the trap. Remove the dishwasher knockout plug if a dishwasher connects.

  • 4

    Wire it. Connect the electrical cable or a plug cord, with a switch or air switch. If there is no outlet, an electrician or plumber adds one.

  • 5

    Test. Run water, check for leaks, then run the unit.

DIY or plumber

Both are reasonable depending on your comfort.

A confident DIY install

  • Swapping a unit in the same location
  • Existing outlet and switch
  • Time to do it carefully

Hire a plumber

  • No existing outlet or switch
  • Drain trap and venting changes
  • Leaks, a dishwasher connection or a cabinet that limits space

Cheshire note: kitchens, food service and grease

Food-service businesses in Cheshire fall under the town's fats, oils and grease program, run through the Water Pollution Control Division. Homes are not covered by that program, though the lesson carries over. Grease from kitchen sinks, even through a disposal, ends up in the same pipes.

Sources: Town of Cheshire Sewer Division and FOG program pages.

Do and do not for a long-lived disposal

Run cold water before, during and after use so fats move through the pipe. Feed small amounts at a time. Skip fibrous vegetables, potato peels in quantity and large bones. A disposal that is overfed will jam, and a jam is the most common call we get about them.

If yours stops working, our garbage disposal repair page explains the reset and wrench steps. If the sink drains slowly with the disposal off, the clog is in the line, and drain cleaning fixes it.

Electrical and switch options

A disposal needs power, usually from an outlet under the sink that stays on, with a wall switch or an air switch to control it. If the sink has a switched outlet already, the install is simple. If it does not, a licensed electrician or plumber adds a circuit or a plug.

An air switch is a button on the counter that sends a puff of air to a controller under the sink, so you do not have a wall switch near water. It is a neat solution in kitchens with no switch location. Whichever you pick, the unit should be on a protected circuit, and the wiring should follow the manufacturer's instructions.

Common installation mistakes

The biggest is forgetting the dishwasher knockout. If a dishwasher connects to the disposal and the plug stays in, the dishwasher cannot drain, and it will back up into the machine. The opposite mistake is knocking out the plug when no dishwasher is attached, which leaves an open port that leaks.

Other mistakes include not tightening the mounting ring evenly, which causes a slow leak at the flange, and using too little putty under the sink flange. Always run water and check every joint after installation, and run the unit with cold water before you walk away.

Cost drivers, in plain terms

The price of an install depends on the unit, the electrical situation and the plumbing connections. A basic swap of a same-size unit costs less than a new install that needs an outlet and a switch. A dishwasher connection, a changed trap or an undersized drain adds time.

We give a clear price before work starts, after confirming the details by phone or at the sink. Tell us the current setup, whether a dishwasher connects and whether an outlet exists under the sink. Photographs of the cabinet help us come prepared with the right parts.

Noise, size and kitchen layout

Under-sink space limits what fits. A tall disposal can crowd a double-bowl sink with a dishwasher and a trap, and in a small cabinet it can block storage you use. Measure the space from the sink flange down to the cabinet floor before you buy.

A sound-insulated unit is quieter but larger. In an open kitchen that trade is often worth it, and in a closed one a standard unit is fine. A short list of measurements saves a return trip to the store.
