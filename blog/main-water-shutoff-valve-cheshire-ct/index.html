Where Is the Main Water Shutoff in a Cheshire Home?

When a pipe bursts, the main water shutoff is the one valve that matters. Find it today, not during the flood.

24/7 emergency line
(203) 720-6269
Call now

By the Cheshire 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

The main water shutoff is a valve where the water line enters your house. In most Connecticut homes it sits in the basement near the front wall, close to the water meter or the pressure regulator. In a slab house it may be in a utility closet, a garage wall or an outside box.

Finding it takes five minutes and can save thousands in water damage. If you do not know where yours is, read on, and then go and look.

Where to look first

Follow the cold water line from the point where it comes through the foundation wall. The first valve you meet is usually the main shutoff. In a basement, check along the front wall nearest the street, around the water heater and near the meter. In a crawlspace, look where the pipe enters from outside.

Some homes have an additional shutoff at the street or curb, owned by the utility. That valve is usually not for homeowners to use. Your own interior shutoff is the one to know.

The two kinds of valve you will find

The handle tells you what you have.

Gate valve (round wheel)

Turn clockwise several times to close. Older gate valves can stick or break, so test yours.

Ball valve (lever handle)

A quarter turn closes it. When the handle sits across the pipe, the valve is closed.

Stop valves at fixtures

Small valves under sinks and behind toilets close water to a single fixture. Use them for local leaks.

Washing machine and water heater valves

Each has its own shutoff. Know where they are as well.

Test it once a year

A valve that has not moved in years can seize or leak when turned.

Cheshire notes: meter, utility and the street

The Regional Water Authority supplies public water in Cheshire. For questions about the water meter or the service line between the street and the house, ask the utility, since those parts are not ours to touch. The RWA's annual water quality report also notes that lead in drinking water comes primarily from service lines and home plumbing, which is a reason to know what your pipes are made of.

Properties on private wells have a different layout. The shutoff may be at the pressure tank, and the well pump breaker is a second thing to turn off. The Chesprocott Health District answers well questions for Cheshire, Prospect and Wolcott.

Sources: Regional Water Authority annual water quality report and Chesprocott Health District website.

What to do when a pipe bursts

Close the main, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines, and call. If you are on a well, switch off the pump breaker as well. Photograph the damage for insurance and keep the area clear. Our burst pipe repair team answers around the clock at (203) 720-6269.

If the valve will not close, shut the street valve only if the utility says you can, or call us and the utility. Do not use a pipe wrench with force, because a broken valve stem releases the whole line.

After the water is off

Open the lowest faucet and then a hot tap to drain the water heater lines. Leave the water heater powered down if the supply is off for long, since a heater without water can be damaged. When the repair is done, open the valve slowly and check for leaks.

If you want a deeper look at what is behind the walls, leak detection finds hidden leaks without tearing out drywall. A planned valve replacement is quick when the water is already off.

Teach the house, and label it

A shutoff nobody can find is only slightly better than none. Walk everyone in the household to the valve, show them which way it turns and let each person try it. Teenagers and guests should know too, because the person home when a pipe bursts is often not the homeowner.

Add a tag with the words main water and an arrow showing the closing direction. If the valve is behind storage, clear a path. A flashlight hung nearby helps at night. Write the location in the same place as your electrical panel notes.

Replacing an old gate valve

Many older homes have a gate valve with a round wheel. Over decades the internal parts corrode, and a valve that has not moved in years may not close fully, or it may break when turned. A ball valve with a lever is a modern replacement that closes with a quarter turn and rarely sticks.

Replacement requires shutting off water upstream, which may mean calling the utility for the curb valve, so schedule it for a time when water can be off for an hour or two. A planned swap is much easier than doing it mid-flood, and it is quick for a plumber.

Seasonal checklist for the valve

Make the valve part of a fall routine. Check that you can reach it, that it turns and that it does not weep at the stem. Look at the pipe near it for green corrosion on copper or flaking on steel. These small clues point to a valve or pipe that will fail under stress.

Before leaving on a long trip in winter, many people close the main and drain the lines, or set the heat to a safe minimum. Decide which you prefer, and tell whoever checks the house. A closed main with the heat on is the safest combination for an empty house.

a shutoff valve swap and the plumbing code

Plumbers in Connecticut work to the 2021 IPC through the 2022 State Building Code. Replacing a main valve is plumbing work, so ask the town whether your job needs a permit. The town Building Official, reachable at 203-271-6640, is the final word on what a Cheshire permit requires.

Nearby towns, same rule

The same steps apply in Meriden, Wallingford and the rest of the area we serve. Teach everyone in the house where the valve is, including teenagers and guests who stay for long visits.
