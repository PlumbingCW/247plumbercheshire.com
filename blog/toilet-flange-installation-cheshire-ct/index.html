Toilet Closet Flange Repair in Cheshire, CT

The closet flange is the small ring that holds your toilet to the floor. When it fails, leaks and rocking follow.

By the Cheshire 24/7 Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

A toilet closet flange is the ring on the floor that connects the toilet to the drain pipe and holds it in place. If the flange cracks, rusts or sits too low, the wax seal fails and the toilet rocks, leaks or smells. Fixing it right means replacing or repairing the flange before setting the toilet again.

This guide explains how the flange works, how to tell if yours is damaged and how it is installed. It also covers why new flooring so often creates flange trouble.

What the flange does

The flange bolts to the floor and sits on top of the drain pipe. Two closet bolts slide into its slots and hold the toilet down. A wax ring or a rubber gasket seals the gap between the toilet and the flange, so waste water goes down the drain and not onto the floor.

Because the flange carries the toilet's weight and every flush, it is exposed to stress. A cast iron flange can rust, a plastic flange can crack and a broken bolt slot leaves nothing to hold the toilet.

Signs the flange is the problem

These symptoms often show up together.

The toilet rocks

Bolts loose, floor soft or flange broken.

Water at the base

The wax ring is not sealing, often because the flange is too low.

Sewer smell in the bathroom

The seal is leaking gas, not only water.

A bolt keeps loosening

The slot is broken or the flange is not fixed to the floor.

Flange height and new flooring

The flange should sit on top of the finished floor, or very close to it.

Too low

  • A thick new floor raises the finished surface above the flange
  • The wax ring cannot compress enough
  • Extra wax rings are a short-term patch, not a fix

Right height

  • Flange rests on the finished floor
  • A standard wax ring seals
  • The toilet sits level and firm

How a flange repair or installation goes

A plumber follows steps like these.

  • 1

    Remove the toilet. Shut the water, drain the tank and bowl, disconnect the supply line and lift the toilet off the bolts.

  • 2

    Inspect the flange and the pipe. Look for cracks, rust, broken slots and a loose pipe.

  • 3

    Repair or replace. A repair ring covers a cracked flange. A badly damaged flange is replaced and fixed to the subfloor.

  • 4

    Set the toilet. New wax ring or gasket, new bolts, level and tighten gradually.

  • 5

    Test. Flush several times and check for leaks.

When to call a plumber

If the flange is cracked, rusted through or loose from the floor, call a plumber, especially if the floor around it feels soft. A damaged subfloor needs repair first. We handle toilet and flange repair across Cheshire, and our toilet repair page covers related fixes.

If you are remodeling, tell the plumber before the new floor goes in, and the flange can be set at the right height. We serve homes in Southington and Meriden too, and a call to (203) 720-6269 starts the scheduling.

Materials: what flanges are made of

Closet flanges come in PVC, ABS, cast iron, brass and stainless steel. PVC and ABS are common in newer homes and are light and inexpensive. Cast iron flanges appear on older cast iron drains and can rust and crack. Brass and stainless flanges resist corrosion and are chosen for repairs.

The flange has to match the drain pipe material for a good connection, and the right fitting depends on whether the flange sits inside or outside the pipe. A plumber checks the pipe and picks the right fit. Mismatched materials can leak at the joint, even when the toilet is set perfectly.

Setting the height after new flooring

Tile, thicker underlayment and heated floors all raise the finished surface. If the flange stays at the old height, it ends up below the new floor, and the wax ring cannot close the gap. The fix is to bring the flange up with an extension ring, or to reset the drain connection at the right height.

Ideally the flange sits on top of the finished floor, or at most a quarter inch above or below it. Tell your tile installer and plumber early in a remodel so the flange is set after the floor, and no one stacks wax rings to make up for it. A thick stack hides a problem that returns within a year.

A sewer smell that returns

A faint sewer smell near a toilet is often a sign of a poor seal at the flange. The wax ring blocks sewer gas as well as water, so when it fails, gas reaches the room before water reaches the floor. If the smell appears and then disappears after you replace the ring, you found it.

If the smell stays after a reset, look at other sources: a dry trap in an unused fixture, a vent problem or a crack in a drain. Pouring a cup of water down a floor drain restores a dry trap. A persistent smell is worth a plumber's visit.

Flange repair kits: when they work

Repair rings and flange extenders can rescue a cracked or low flange without a full replacement. They suit a flange that is solid but cracked at the top, or a flange that is slightly low. They are not suited to a flange that is corroded through, or a drain pipe that is loose.

If a kit is the right choice, the fix is fast, and it avoids cutting into the floor. A plumber can tell you which type you have in a few minutes after the toilet is lifted.
