Sewer camera inspection in Cheshire, CT is a planned way to look inside a drain or sewer line before you decide what to fix. Our line is open 24/7 for urgent backups, and inspections are scheduled when the schedule allows. A waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels through the pipe and sends live video to a monitor.

The camera shows pipe material, joints, roots, cracks, offsets, low spots that hold water and debris. Most people schedule one for a recurring clog, before buying a house, or before spending money on a repair.

When an inspection is worth it

The simplest rule is to inspect before you spend. If cabling has not stopped the backups, the camera tells you whether you need a liner, a spot repair, or only a better cleaning schedule. If you are buying an older Cheshire house, an inspection of the sewer lateral shows a risk that a standard home inspection often misses.

A camera is also the right tool after a repair. It confirms the pipe is clear and the joints are sealed, so the work is verified rather than assumed. If the camera cannot pass a blockage, we clean first, then run it again.

What the camera finds

These are the findings that change a repair decision.

Roots

Fine roots at joints, or a dense mass. Both point to a repair or a root-control plan.

Cracks and offsets

Joints that have shifted or pipe that has broken. Offsets catch paper and cause repeat clogs.

Bellies

A low spot that holds water and solids, often caused by soil settling under the pipe.

Scale and grease

Deposits on the walls that narrow the pipe. These suit hydro jetting.

Pipe material

Cast iron, clay, PVC or orangeburg. Material predicts how the pipe ages.

Buying an older house in Cheshire

Cheshire's older neighborhoods, including streets near the Cheshire Historic District and the Route 10 corridor, may have original clay or cast iron laterals. A camera inspection before closing can show the real condition of a line that cannot be seen from the basement.

If the house is on septic, the picture is different. A camera can still examine the line between house and tank, though the tank, baffles and drain field are inspected separately. The Chesprocott Health District, which serves Cheshire, Prospect and Wolcott, answers septic questions for the area.

How an inspection works

The visit is straightforward.

  • 1

    Access. We find the cleanout. If there is none, we discuss another entry point.

  • 2

    Run the camera. We feed the camera through the line while recording.

  • 3

    Mark the findings. We note depth, distance and any damage, and locate issues from above when possible.

  • 4

    Review the video. We explain what we saw and give you the recording.
