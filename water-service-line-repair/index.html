The water service line is the buried pipe that carries drinking water from the utility's main in the street into your home. It crosses your yard, passes through the foundation and ends at the main shutoff and meter. A repair replaces the failed section or the whole line.

When a water line break is an emergency

A full break floods a yard or basement quickly and leaves the house without water. A slow leak is quieter and sneakier, showing up first as a bill that climbs, a meter that spins with everything off, or pressure that sags in the evening. Both are worth a same-day call.

If water pools near the foundation, shut the main and watch the basement. For a line that has failed in a front lawn along a busy street, a wet patch that appears in dry weather is the strongest clue. Call 811 before anyone digs, since gas, electric and communication lines share the ground.

Signs the service line is the problem

The service line is easy to blame and easy to miss. These clues help.

A wet or green patch with no rain

Leaking water feeds soil and grass above the line.

Low pressure at every fixture

A partial break or a corroded line reduces flow to the whole house.

A meter that moves with everything off

Water is leaving the house side of the meter, and a buried line is a prime suspect.

Discolored water or sediment

Corroded older pipe can shed rust and scale.

Sound of running water near the meter

A leak close to the entry point can be heard at the main.

Who owns which part of the line

Cheshire's public water comes from the Regional Water Authority. The utility maintains its main in the street, and the homeowner is generally responsible for the service pipe on the property. The exact point where responsibility changes depends on the utility's rules, so ask the Regional Water Authority about your address before ordering a repair.

Some Cheshire properties, and many homes in Prospect, use private wells instead. A private well has no service line in this sense, and the equivalent problems are in the line from the well to the house. The Chesprocott Health District serves Cheshire, Prospect and Wolcott and answers well questions.

Replace the whole line or repair one spot

A plumber weighs age, material and how often the line has failed.

Spot repair

  • Fits a single break on an otherwise sound pipe
  • Lowest cost and shortest dig
  • May leave other weak spots in an old line

Full replacement

  • Fits corroded, undersized or repeatedly failing lines
  • Resets the clock with new copper or approved plastic pipe
  • Longer job, and can sometimes be done with fewer trenches

Safety steps before a plumber arrives

A failed service line can flood a yard or basement fast. Find your main shutoff valve and close it if water is entering the house, then call the 24/7 line.

If water is surfacing in the yard, keep people and pets off the soft ground. Call 811 before anyone digs, and do not dig near the line yourself.
