Repiping in Cheshire, CT replaces the water supply lines inside a home when they have worn out. It is a planned project, and our 24/7 line is open if a failing pipe turns urgent. If a pipe is spraying now, shut off the main and call us.

A repipe swaps old galvanized steel, leaking copper or brittle plastic for new copper or PEX throughout the house, or for the worst section only. The goal is steady pressure, clean water and an end to chasing one leak after another.

When repiping makes sense

One leak is a repair. A second and third leak in the same system within a year or two is a pattern. Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside, narrowing the pipe until pressure drops and water runs brown. Copper can develop pinholes, especially where water chemistry or flow speed attacks the walls.

The point is cost over time. Repeated emergency calls, wall repairs and water damage add up, and a repipe resolves them in one project. A plumber looks at the pipe material, the failure history and the layout before recommending a full or partial job.

Signs the pipes are at the end of their life

These signs appear in homes of many ages.

Brown or rusty water

Corroding galvanized or iron pipe sheds rust, most noticeably after the water has sat.

Low pressure throughout the house

Mineral buildup and corrosion shrink the opening inside old pipe.

Repeated pinhole leaks

Small blue-green stains or spray from copper pipe point to wall thinning.

Visible corrosion at joints

Green crust on copper or flaking on steel signals wear.

A mix of old and new repairs

Patchwork piping fails at the joints between materials.

Older Cheshire homes and pipe materials

Cheshire includes homes from many eras, from houses near the Cheshire Historic District to newer subdivisions. Houses built before the later decades of the twentieth century may still have galvanized steel supply lines somewhere, often hidden in walls or the basement ceiling. Look at the pipes where they enter the house and at the main shutoff, and note the color and material.

We do not guess the pipe material of your home from its age, because renovations change it. A short visit to look at exposed pipes in the basement tells more than any assumption. Water test results are separate, and the Regional Water Authority publishes its annual water quality report if you want to see what it says about lead and other measures.

PEX or copper for the new pipes

Both are accepted choices. The right one depends on the house and your priorities.

PEX

  • Flexible, fed through walls with fewer cuts
  • Resists bursting from freezing better than rigid pipe
  • Faster install in many houses

Copper

  • A long track record and rigid, neat runs
  • Handles high temperatures and is easy to inspect
  • Costs more in material and labor

Permits and code for repiping

The 2022 Connecticut State Building Code is built on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Replacing the supply piping through a house is a major job that normally calls for a permit conversation up front. If your job might need a permit, call the Cheshire Building Department at 203-271-6640 before work starts.
