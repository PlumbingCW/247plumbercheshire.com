Water heater installation in Cheshire, CT is the planned version of the job. The old tank is still working, or you are adding a unit to a new space, and there is time to choose. Our line is open 24/7, so you can call any time to schedule a visit.

An installation covers the heater, its water connections, the gas line or electrical circuit, the vent or condensate path, the relief valve and the drain. Done well, it should run safely and quietly, and be easy to service for years.

Choosing a type: tank, tankless or heat pump

A standard tank is the most common choice. It holds heated water in reserve, costs the least up front, and replaces easily. A tankless unit heats water only on demand and takes less floor space, though it needs the right gas supply, venting and often a larger gas line. A heat pump water heater moves heat from the surrounding air and uses less electricity, and it needs space and airflow around it.

The fit depends on the household. A family with several morning showers will notice recovery time. A small household may prefer low running costs. We look at your existing gas, electric and venting before recommending anything, so the new unit does not need a surprise upgrade.

How an installation goes

Here is the usual sequence for a planned install.

  • 1

    Walk-through. We look at the current heater, the gas or electric supply, the vent and the space around it.

  • 2

    Sizing and options. We recommend a unit, explain what changes, and give a clear price before work starts.

  • 3

    Install day. We drain and remove the old unit, set the new one, connect water, fuel and venting, and fit a relief valve and drain.

  • 4

    Testing. We check for leaks, test combustion on gas models, and show you the shutoff valves.

Cheshire details that affect an install

Many Cheshire homes have basement or garage installations, and an older house near the Cheshire Historic District may have tight stairs or low ceilings that limit unit size. A tape measure at the install location saves surprises, so measure height and clearance before choosing a model.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Cheshire Building Department at 84 South Main Street whether your job needs one. The Building Official issues plumbing permits, and a licensed plumber can handle it for you. For gas appliances, the venting and combustion air matter as much as the heater, so we test them on the visit.
